The one-line pitch

You're a doggo working the night shift at a facility that contains things it probably shouldn't. You have your kit. Your kit does not save you.

Why this isn't just another fangame

There are a lot of DB fangames. They're all on Scratch, which means they're all competing on the same thing — who can build the best Scratch project — and they're all hitting the same ceiling.

That ceiling isn't anyone's fault. Re'ruffed is enormous and it lags because Scratch lags, not because IMakeFunni did anything wrong. Scratch cloud variables are ten numbers, they're slow, and they can't carry a real multiplayer game. Everyone is building brilliant things inside a box.

This one isn't in the box. It's a web app, and that changes what's possible, not just what's smooth:

Scratch can't Real online multiplayer

Cloud vars are 10 numbers and slow. Nobody has ever played DB with a friend over the internet. Not "it'd be laggy" — it can't be done.

Scratch can't A building you explore

Big spaces with state that persists as you move through them. Doors that stay open. Lights that stay off.

Scratch can't Lots of stuff at once

The wall Re'ruffed hit. Dozens of things moving, still 60fps.

The actual idea

Here's the part that makes this worth building instead of just being "DB but bigger."

In every DB game, combat is the game. You fight, you win, that's the loop. In Operation B.A.R.K., combat is a resource.

You keep everything you already know — the kit, the specials, the med-kits dropping on a timer. All of it works exactly like it does in DB. But none of it kills the things in the facility. What it does is buy you seconds.

  • A mallet swing staggers it — just long enough to get through a door.
  • A special knocks it back — and burns a cooldown you're going to regret.
  • A med-kit heals you — and the noise brings something else.

You never win the fight. You pay to survive it. Which means every skill anyone already has from DB still matters, and you're still terrified, and those two things have never been true at the same time in this franchise.

Why anyone plays it twice

Horror games die on replay — you learn where the scares are and it deflates. Three fixes, and they stack:

The big one Bring friends

Terrifying alone, unbelievably stupid with four people. Nobody replays horror for the monster — they replay it for what their friend did when the monster showed up. This is the multiplayer Scratch literally cannot do.

Structural Shuffle the building

Layout and which anomalies are awake change every run. You can't memorize a route that isn't there.

Structural Knowledge is the upgrade

No stat tree. You learn that this one hunts by sound and that one freezes if you look at it. Run five is easier because you got better.
